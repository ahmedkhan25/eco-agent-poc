An early partner project · City of Logan, Queensland

A fast-growing city, shaped by water

Logan sits between Brisbane and the Gold Coast. It is one of Queensland's fastest-growing cities, and two rivers and around 90 creeks run through it. Every growth decision is also a water decision.

The context

Floods are part of Logan's story

The Logan and Albert rivers have flooded many times in living memory. Council already runs strong tools for this: a Disaster Dashboard, early warning alerts, and a property-level Flood Portal.

  • 2022 was the most significant Logan River flood since 1974.
  • 2025 brought moderate to major flooding from ex-Tropical Cyclone Alfred.
  • Council's mapping uses the 1-in-100 year (1% AEP) event. Anything we show follows that framing.

The problem we can help with

Big decisions, scattered evidence

Council is planning for about 100,000 more residents. The evidence for each choice lives in different places: flood studies, census data, river gauges, asset plans, and community feedback.

  • Community Infrastructure Plan 2024–2041: where do new libraries, centres and pools go?
  • Corporate Plan 2026–2031: drainage, overland flow, asset management, growth.
  • Disaster and climate plans: flood, bushfire, heatwave and storms.

Where ecoheart fits

We add to Council's tools. We don't replace them.

ecoheart is a decision layer that sits on top of the mapping and data Council already trusts. It brings the evidence together, answers plain-language questions with sources, and lets staff test "what if" options. People make the decisions.

  • Human-first: AI speeds up the analysis; staff stay in charge.
  • Explainable: every answer shows where it came from.
  • Consistent: uses Council's own flood framing and versions.

The first use case

One catchment, one clear question

We'd start small: flood and infrastructure planning in one catchment, such as the lower Logan River around Eagleby and Beenleigh. The goal is to answer one real planning question well.

  • Example question: "Which community facilities planned for this area sit inside the 1% AEP flood extent, and how many residents rely on them?"
  • Answer on a map, with a short summary and every source listed.
  • Staff review, adjust and decide.

Illustrative example. The question would be agreed with Council.

The data

Start with public data only

The first stage uses only published Australian data. It needs no personal information and no connection to Council systems. Tap a card for what each source gives us.

FloodCheck is regional modelled mapping, not live floodwater. Only Council's Flood Portal is property-level.

Timeline

A realistic path, with a check at each step

A public-data demo in the first month is realistic. Working with Council-held data takes longer, because of procurement, privacy and security review, and staff time. We plan for one to three months and agree each step in writing.

Not in scope for any early stage: live emergency operations or automated alerts.
